## Verdict (read this first)

The wedge is the right SIZE and aimed at the wrong BUYER. Build it almost unchanged, but re-aim roughly half the demand test at professional/research-heavy knowledge workers, because the plan's own category analysis ("consumers don't pay for browsers; professional/enterprise knowledge workers do") contradicts what its success criteria actually measure: consumer-style retention of five hand-picked enthusiasts. A GREEN result under the current gate tells you the product delights friends of the founder. It tells you almost nothing about whether any path to $8M liquid exists. That mismatch is fixable with about two days of instrumentation and recruiting choices, zero additional build scope.

Recommendation: **REVISE (minor)**. Six specific amendments, all cheap, none structural. List in Implementation Tasks below. With those landed, this plan is among the more honest small-bet designs this reviewer has read: written kill criteria, denominators fixed in advance, slip policy that cuts scope instead of stretching gates, and an afternoon-cost demand probe scheduled BEFORE five agent-weeks of building.

## 0A. Premise Challenge

### P1. The wedge tests engagement, not the business. (WARNING, structural)

Stated objective function: ~$8M liquid, else trash the project. The wedge measures: do 5 non-founder humans use a free local capture-and-ask browser weekly. These are different questions. The plan knows this ("GREEN does not mean the consumer browser is a business") and routes GREEN to a written decision memo on revenue paths. Good. But then the instrumentation never collects anything that would let that memo conclude anything except "people like it." Domain-class usage, job-context of captures, stated willingness to pay, and compliance sensitivity are all absent from Measurement. See the arithmetic section below and amendment T1.

### P2. Six of nine core flows buy zero information for the demand question. (OK with eyes open)

Tabs, omnibox, back/forward, bookmarks, search, session restore are commodity browser features. They exist in the plan because (a) the long-term product is a browser and (b) v1 died precisely on chrome unusability, so chrome viability is itself a restart thesis. That reasoning is sound and the W2 kill trigger honors it. The cost is honest to state: roughly 60% of the build weeks serve the BROWSER hypothesis, roughly 40% serves the MEMORY hypothesis, while the demand gate only scores the memory side. Acceptable, provided shell risk gets the escape hatch in P3.

### P3. The W2 pivot menu is missing its best option. (FINDING)

If shell-plus-bridge is not green by end of W2, the plan offers: fork Zen, deeper salvage, or kill. Missing: pivot to a companion app (menu-bar capture-and-ask over Safari/Chrome via share-sheet or extension intake), which preserves the entire capture/store/cite stack, drops the chrome risk entirely, and still answers the demand question. It decouples demand learning from shell risk. Effort to ADD TO THE PLAN: one sentence. Amendment T2.

### P4. Status-quo competition is misidentified. (WARNING)

"Nobody else has been observed solving this" treats AI browsers as the competitor set. For the behavior being tested, the incumbent is duct tape: Save-to-Notion/Obsidian/Readwise plus pasting context into ChatGPT. People who feel this pain already patched it, imperfectly. Two consequences:
1. The Assignment should ask "what do you do today when you want to remember something you read?" not only "want early access?" The answer qualifies the tester and hands you competitive intel for free. Amendment T3.
2. Your differentiation versus duct tape is zero-setup and verifiable citations, not raw capability. The demo must show the click-through-citation moment, because that is the part Notion saves cannot do.

### P5. Local-only is asserted as differentiator, never tested as purchase reason. (FINDING)

Privacy and $0 marginal cost justify the architecture. They may also BE the pitch for the paying segment: professionals whose material cannot go to a cloud (legal, finance, health, defense-adjacent). Nothing in Measurement asks whether local-only matters for the tester's job. One check-in question. Amendment T1 includes it.

### P6. If we do nothing: pain persists daily for exactly one person. (HONEST)

Founder pain is real and daily. External pain is unproven. The plan says so plainly and prices the proof at one afternoon (The Assignment). That assignment is the best paragraph in the document. Strengthen it per T3, do not skip it, do not write code before replies are counted.

### P7. Timing cut, both directions. (CONTEXT)

OpenAI retiring Atlas this month creates refugee traffic (Arc/Atlas/Comet users) actively shopping for a browser home. Tailwind for recruiting. Counter-risk: Dia is Atlassian-funded and could ship local memory or a free tier during your 11-13 week cycle; your moat is being local-first and citation-honest from day one, so speed matters more than polish.

## The $8M arithmetic (objective-function audit)

The plan ties every gate to the objective function rhetorically. Here is the actual math, which the plan never writes down and should.

Paths to ~$8M liquid for a solo founder:

| Path | Arithmetic | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Consumer subscription, acquired at ~4x ARR | Need ~$2M ARR. At $20/mo that is ~8,300 paid seats. At typical 2-5% free-to-paid conversion, that is 170K-420K free installs. | Distribution problem 100x larger than this test can inform. Possible in theory, invisible from a 50-person cohort. |
| Professional/team tier | $30/seat/mo x e.g. 500 teams x 20 seats = ~$3.6M ARR. Needs either PLG engine or founder-led sales motion. | The only path where a SOLO founder's leverage (product + agents, no payroll) plausibly competes. Also the only path this test currently fails to instrument. |
| YC application | YC trades ~$500K for 7%; it buys fundraising optionality, not liquidity. Liquidity then depends on raising at traction-implied valuations or selling. | An accelerant of whichever path above shows signal. Not a path by itself. |
| Bootstrap profit to $8M | Even $50K/mo take-home takes ~13 years. | Violates the founder's own timeline instincts; not credible. |
| acqui-hire or tech pickup | Solo founder + working local-AI browser stack post-Atlas-retirement has modest talent value; tech value depends on proof anyone wants the memory layer. | Realistic only AFTER demand evidence exists. Another reason the wedge must generate legible evidence. |

Conclusions:
1. No outcome of THIS test can validate reachability of $8M. Say so in the plan so GREEN is never misread as "on track." What the test CAN do: falsify the consumer-memory thesis cheaply (~11-13 weeks total), and detect whether professional/compliance pull exists. Both are worth far more than they cost.
2. Therefore the test's highest-value output is the PROFESSIONAL signal, and the current gate measures none of it. This is the single biggest strategic inconsistency in the plan, and fixing it costs days, not weeks.
3. Statistical honesty: n=50 with 20% install target gives a 95% confidence interval of roughly +/-11 points. These gates are directional tripwires for kill decisions, which is their correct use. They are NOT measurements suitable for extrapolation, fundraising decks, or self-congratulation. The plan's own "no public landing-page counter" instinct should extend to private interpretation of these numbers.

## Review sections (all eleven evaluated; findings only, compressed for a strategy-stage plan)

### 1. Architecture

```
[Web chrome (single layer)] --typed JSON bridge--> [Native host]
        |                                              |-- tabs/nav/omnibox/bookmarks/session
        |                                              |-- capture.request -> readability extract
        |                                                      -> SQLite+FTS5 (spans, offsets, hash-versioned)
        '-- ask.request ----> [Retriever BM25] --> [Local MLX model]
                                |                      |-- validate-and-retry (second constrained pass)
                                '<-- stream chunks ----' '-- refuse if no span clears relevance
Counters (opt-in, class-only) --> Cloudflare Worker (free tier)
```

- Contract test as merge gate: directly closes the known v1 defect. OK.
- Single point of failure: extraction quality gates corpus quality gates answer quality. The raw-visible-text fallback handles the loud case; the QUIET case is mediocre extractions that pass and poison retrieval. Mitigation already partly present (golden set). Add per-capture extraction-confidence logging so bad-extraction clusters are visible. (Task T6)
- Rollback posture: pre-launch, versioned recapture handles schema evolution; archive stays archived; reversibility 5/5. OK.

### 3. Security & Threat Model

- Read-cite attack surface: prompt injection via captured text is the whole model. Plan's mitigations (delimited untrusted data, output contract with no actions, adversarial fixtures in CI) match Trail of Bits/UW guidance for this class. OK.
- Residual: authenticated-page content captured at user request lands PLAINTEXT in SQLite. For the professional cohort E1 recruits (lawyers, finance), that matters. Cheap hardening: rely on FileVault explicitly in docs, or encrypt the capture DB with a keychain key. Decide in W1; one-day item. (WARNING, task folded into T6)
- Tokenized links leak only install attribution; founder keeps mapping by hand. Proportionate. OK.
- No secrets, no cloud inference, no background fetches. Posture consistent. OK.

### 4. Data Flow & Interaction Edge Cases

Handled in plan: failed extraction fallback UI, dedup by normalized URL + content hash, versioned recapture with latest-wins, SPA viewport-only limitation disclosed. Gaps:
- Zero-capture first-run: fresh tester opens Ask with empty corpus. The refusal UX and an empty-state ("capture 3 pages to start asking") decide whether day-1 feels magic or broken. Specify the empty state now. (T7)
- Double-click capture, capture-during-navigation-away, recapture-mid-ask: standard races; cover in the nine-flow E2E set. Minor.
- Golden set is built from the FOUNDER's captures; tester corpora will differ in genre. Check-ins must specifically probe ask-quality on tester-shaped content, not just satisfaction. Folded into T1 wording.

### 5. Code Quality (plan-stage)

- Complexity smell check: one chrome layer, one store, one model path. Moving parts count passes.
- Bookmark flow is the weakest-scoped core flow (zero demand information). First candidate under the slip policy's scope-cut review. Note, do not mandate.
- Naming/schema: span-offset storage is clean and future-proof. OK.

### 6. Test Review

- Nine-flow CDP E2E + bridge contract as merge gates: right ambition, correct rejection of unit-count vanity metrics.
- Gap: golden-set HARNESS should exist by end of W3 (subset scoring already planned W3) so W4's full-gate run is calibration, not construction. Make it explicit. (T8)
- Gap: memory-pressure/degradation test on the M1 8GB floor belongs in CI-adjacent manual protocol from W2. (T5)
- Chaos question: kill the app mid-generation; relaunch restores session and corpus intact (session.restore is already a bridge message). Cover in E2E. Minor.

### 7. Performance

- Floor machine named (M1 Air 8GB) with ALL gates run on it: excellent and rare.
- Missing: named latency budgets for THE core moment. Proposal: cold first ask <=15s including model load; warm ask <=3s to first streamed token; capture <=2s. Without numbers, "fast enough" becomes taste, and taste lost v1. (T5)
- Memory arithmetic on 8GB: macOS idle 3-4GB + CEF/webviews 1-2GB + 4-bit 4B model ~2.5-3GB + KV cache. Feasible only WITH the degradation spec from Section 2. This is the plan's biggest unpriced technical risk. WARNING.

### 8. Observability

### 9. Deployment & Rollout
